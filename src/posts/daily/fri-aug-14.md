### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)	

> Favouritism is wholly inconsistent with discipleship to the Lord.
> 
> The crowning truth, nevertheless, is that 'Mercy triumphs over judgement!' (2:13). This is not to say that mercy and judgement are in contradiction of one another in the mind and purpose of God. On the contrary, they are in perfect harmony because of the perfect sacrifice of Jesus Christ. And this is reflected in the way the Christian handles mercy and judgement. Mercy points to the cross. The believer loves his Saviour and therefore, like him, delights in mercy. But he can also look judgement in the face, as he trusts in the mercy of Christ with a holy confidence. The delightful paradox is that the one who lives (1) according to the law of liberty and (2) as one who is being judged is precisely the person who enjoys the practice of godliness and the quiet assurance of salvation! Mercy 'triumphs' in the Christian's life, because the Christian salvation is both mercy received and mercy lived out-and all because of the infinite love and mercy of God in Jesus Christ! Having been so blessed with the unmerited free grace of God, the Christian cannot but recoil from the evil of discrimination in the church, whether on economic, social, racial or any other grounds. Mercy truly received issues in mercy unconditionally shared.

Gordon J. Keddie, The Practical Christian: The Message of James, Welwyn Commentary Series
