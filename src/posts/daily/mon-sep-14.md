### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)	

The angels in Luke 2 announce to the shepherds the glorious Good News of the birth of Jesus—the Savior, Messiah, and Lord. This good news had been anticipated ever since the Garden of Eden. But the good news always comes in the context of bad news. Here in Genesis 3, sin has destroyed the beauty of the “all very good” creation (Gen 1:31).

After listening to the serpent (later identified as the devil and Satan Rev 12:9) and believing his lie about God, the man and his wife took of the forbidden fruit and ate it, plunging the world into sin and misery. They were then afraid of God and hid from Him. They were ashamed of their nakedness. And they began to blame others for their sin. But in that context of bad news and misery, good news comes in Gen 3:15 as God curses the serpent.

As Ferguson puts it, _The curse is really this: “There’s going to be enmity between your seed and the seed of the woman, Eve, and there is going to be conflict that will go on until the seed of the woman crushes your head.”_ And in that curse, there is a promise that Christ will come and defeat the power of sin and Satan. That is good news!
