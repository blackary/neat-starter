### Notes

(See below for all authors.)	

> This is the first of Isaiah's four Servant Songs, fulfilled in Christ. He is the servant of the Lord. He is God; alternative to our idols. He is not an abomination; he is a delight. But he also stands in contrast with Cyrus the conqueror who "shall trample on rulers as on mortar, as the potter treads clay" (41:25). Cyrus stepped on people. But when Jesus rose in history, he did not break a bruised reed or quench a faintly burning wick. When Matthew quotes this text in the New Testament, he says it was fulfilled when Jesus was healing sick people, and Jesus was quiet about it (Matthew 12:15-21). He gave suffering people their lives back, and he didn't use his success with them to take advantage of them or promote himself. No destructive swagger. No brutal grasping. A gentle servant brings forth justice to the nations.
   
Raymond C. Ortlund Jr., Isaiah: God Saves Sinners, Preaching the Word
