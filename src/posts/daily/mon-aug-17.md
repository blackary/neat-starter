### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)

> How we treat the rich and the poor indicates our individual and corporate discipleship. James points out the sinful tendency to be attracted to those who are rich, famous, glamorous, beautiful, and powerful, while subtly despising those who lack the marks of social status. He introduces the topic with an interesting Greek construction that literally calls to us as believers in "our Lord Jesus Christ, the Glory." This provides us with an unusual title for our Savior.
> 
> In the Bible, God's glory is sometimes manifested in the cloud of angels and people gathered around him. God's glory-cloud accompanies beautiful lights and great sounds. Moreover, God's glory shades and protects his people. If Jesus is the Glory, the rich and powerful of this world are nothing in comparison. We need not curry favor with the fading glory of the wealthy and mighty. We already have favor with Christ the Glory.
> 
> James writes that we need to use the eyes of faith to discern true glory. Anyone united to Christ the Glory enjoys riches and power far beyond that of any politician or wealthy person. The man or woman without earthly money and nice clothes has true glory.

R.C. Sproul, *Before the Face of God: Book 4: A Daily Guide for Living from Ephesians, Hebrews, and James.*
