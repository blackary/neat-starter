**WSC #94**

_What is baptism?_

Baptism is a sacrament, wherein the washing with water in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost, doth signify and seal our ingrafting into Christ, and partaking of the benefits of the covenant of grace, and our engagement to be the Lord’s.

<div><small><i>Matt. 28:19; Rom. 6:4; Gal. 3:27</i></small></div>

### Notes

(See below for all authors.)	

Why was Jesus baptized? Matthew’s gospel accounts tell us that it was “to fulfil all righteousness” (Matt 3:15). Jesus, in insisting on being baptized by John is identifying with John’s message—repent and be baptized. Jesus, of course, had no sin in Himself. But he became sin (2 Cor 5:21) for all those the Father had given Him (John 6:37). In His baptism Jesus takes on His role as Messiah, whereby He takes on Himself the sin and punishment of His people (Is 53:5-6).

As Jesus was baptized, He was praying. Ryle states that _a baptism without prayer, it may be confidently asserted, is a baptism on which we have no right to expect God’s blessing_. Luke speaks often of Jesus praying (Lu 5:15-16, 6:12, 9:18, 9:28, 10:21, 11:1, 22:32, 22:40, 23:46, 24:30). It’s clear this was a regular part of Jesus’s life and ministry. And it is to be regular in the life of Jesus’s disciples.

As Jesus was praying, the heavens opened and the three-in-one God was there. The Holy Spirit came down from heaven in the physical form of a dove. And the voice of God the Father spoke from heaven declaring His pleasure in His much-loved Son.

Just a short account in Luke, but certainly a Wow! moment.
