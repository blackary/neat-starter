_What is baptism?_

Baptism is a sacrament, wherein the washing with water in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost, doth signify and seal our ingrafting into Christ, and partaking of the benefits of the covenant of grace, and our engagement to be the Lord’s.

### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)	

Luke shows us here the beginning of John’s public ministry. He identifies the public figures of the day, but focuses on John, son of Zechariah and Elizabeth. He was in the wilderness where Luke had told us he was until his public ministry began (Lu 1:80). As soon as he heard from God, he began to proclaim Jesus.

Isaiah had told us that this would happen just like Luke now said these things were being accomplished as he started his gospel (Lu 1:1) account.

As John worked, he told people they were going the wrong way on the road. _Turn around_, he said. _Repent_. To repent is to change our mind and our lives—to turn away from sin and turn to God—the One who leads us to repentance by His kindness (Rom 2:4). And all who are granted repentance by God’s grace will hate sin and turn to God.

But some were just coming for the show. They wanted to get a little bit wet and be a little bit good. John said they were like poisonous snakes. Instead, show your repentance through your fruit. Unless your fruit is good, you are yourself bad (Lu 6:43-44).

John assured these repentant, baptized people that Jesus would improve their baptism. After Jesus’s earthly ministry was completed, He will baptize His disciples with the Sprit and with fire (Acts 2:3-4).
