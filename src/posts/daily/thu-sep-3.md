### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)	

If the Lord is willing, we will be considering Luke, then Acts as the preaching content over the next year or so at SRC. Some talk about these two books as 1st and 2nd Luke. Both were, we believe, written by a Gentile physician (Col 4:14) named Luke. Both were dedicated to Theophilus, a name that means *lover of God* or *loved by God*. Most excellent is often used to speak of a high-ranking official (Acts 23:26; 24:2; 26:25).

Luke begins by telling Theophilus why he wrote this gospel account. Others had written as eyewitnesses of the events that had happened—things about the life and ministry of Jesus Christ. Even though Luke wasn’t an eyewitness, he set out to write an orderly account of these things. As Stivason notes, *Luke investigated. He interviewed. He compared sources. He examined testimony*.

And all of this had a purpose—that Theophilus (and all readers of this gospel account) would have certainty about the truth of this good news about Jesus. Christianity is based on objective truth. Truth about Jesus the Christ (the Messiah). The One who always was as God the Son, who became human, who lived an observable life on this earth some 2,000 years ago, who was cruelly and unjustly killed, and who was raised from the dead and taken up into heaven. This Jesus offers forgiveness of sins and everlasting life for all who believe in Him. Do you believe in Him?
