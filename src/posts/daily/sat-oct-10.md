### Catechism

**WSC 85**

_What doth God require of us, that we may escape his wrath and curse, due to us for sin?_

To escape the wrath and curse of God, due to us for sin, God requireth of us faith in Jesus Christ, repentance unto life, with the diligent use of all the outward means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption.

### Notes

(See below for all authors.)	

Jesus met another sinner—a tax collector named Levi. Matthew 9:9 tells us that his other name was Matthew. As far as we know, Luke’s Levi is Matthew. Matthew the gospel writer was Levi the tax collector. Tax collectors were hated in Jesus’s day. They served Rome who held Israel under their authority. And tax collectors of this day often collected more than they were supposed to (Luke 3:12–13) and probably kept the difference for themselves.

Everybody knew that tax collectors were sinners. So much so, that “tax collectors and sinners” were often said in the same breath. But Jesus came to forgive sinners. So, He called Levi to repent, forgave him, and called him to follow Jesus. So, Levi did. He was so thankful, that he held a huge feast for Jesus and his other tax-collector friends. The Pharisees and scribes were upset.

Why would Jesus, who claimed to be righteous, eat with “tax collectors and sinners”? Jesus did this because He came to call sinners to repentance and to forgive their sins. The Pharisees needed their sins forgiven, too, but they denied it. They claimed to keep the Old Testament law perfectly. So, of course, they thought they didn’t need to be forgiven. But they did. They needed new life in Jesus. The old way, even though it was God’s way, wasn’t enough.
