### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)	

We’re told that Simeon, a righteous and devout man, was waiting for the consolation (or comfort) of Israel. He knew that comfort would come through the promised Messiah.

Isaiah prophesies God’s consolation to His people in Babylon. But, because this happens 100 years or so after Isaiah writes, some claim a “2nd Isaiah” as the author of this section. But the Bible presents Isaiah as a unified book with one author—Isaiah, son of Amoz.

So, by the Spirit of God, Isaiah writes above his ability and brings comfort to Judah—and to us. Comfort—yes, comfort. How gracious is our God! To be sure, we sin. And we often face the temporal consequences of our sin as discipline from a loving Father. But an end to God’s loving discipline comes. And comfort from God comes tenderly to His people.

The promised comfort was to come through a Shepherd who would tenderly care for His flock (v.11). This comforting Shepherd is the child Simeon took up in his arms. He would purchase God’s pardon for our sin with His own life. God is justly angry with us when we sin—but only temporarily. His ultimate wrath has been poured out on Christ for us.
