### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)	

While Jesus’s public ministry is expanding, John the Baptist is in prison (Luke 1:18-19). Even though John had willingly said that Jesus’s ministry would increase and he would decrease (John 3:30), John was beginning to doubt if Jesus really was the promised Messiah.

Hardship can do that to us. Satan constantly tempts us to think that if God really cared for us, nothing hard would happen to us. Sometimes we doubt, like John did. When we doubt, we should bring our doubt to Jesus, like John did. Jesus assured John that He is the Messiah and His miracles and His gospel preaching shows that.

Jesus went on to affirm to the crowds the greatness of John as the one sent by God to be a messenger about Jesus. John’s baptism, which we read about back in chapter 3, was calling sinners to repent. Tax collectors and sinners repented and believed God’s way of righteousness (v. 29). That way is a righteousness provided for us by faith. Jesus’s perfect righteousness is given to all who believe. But some reject God’s way—thinking that their righteousness is enough. Don’t be one of them!

Jesus came to be a friend of tax collectors and sinners. Sinners like me. Sinners like you.
