### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)	

A voice is crying in the wilderness. What voice? And what is it crying? Though not identified by Isaiah directly, this voice must have been first faithful prophets with the exiles in Babylon. They called out to King Cyrus to release God’s people and prepare a way for them to return to their land. And he did (Isaiah 45:1-13, Ezra 1:1-4).

Luke, and the other New Testament gospel writers, though, also identified this voice as that of John the Baptist. He would announce the Lord coming to lead His people to full salvation.

And what did the voice cry? Make a highway for God! Smooth out the terrain! This will reveal God’s glory! And John the evangelist would write, we beheld His glory (John 1:14)!

Do we consider enough the glory of God revealed in Jesus for the salvation of His people? God in the flesh walking beside sinful men and women; living a perfect life; dying a horrible death – not for His own failures, but for the sins of all whom the Father had given Him—and returning to His Father to prepare a (high)way for us to be with Him. And we can count on it. God has spoken. We are as unreliable as grass that dries up, but His word is eternally true.
