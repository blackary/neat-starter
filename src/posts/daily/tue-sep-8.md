### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)

In Luke 1, Mary magnifies the Lord (Lu 1:46) and Zechariah blesses the Lord God (Lu 1:68). When we magnify God, we don’t make Him bigger—but as we declare His works He gets bigger in our minds. But we usually think of blessing as God granting His favor to us. The lesser is blessed by the greater (Heb 7:7). Motyer makes a helpful distinction: _When the Lord ‘blesses’ us, he reviews our needs and responds to them; when we ‘bless’ the Lord, we review his excellencies and respond to them._

In Psalm 103, with King David, and ultimately with King Jesus, we join in blessing God from all that is inside us—with our very souls. Like Mary we bless God for His saving, redeeming work. We bless God for His mercy and faithful, steadfast love to those who fear Him. We bless God for satisfying us with good things.

Like Zechariah, we bless God for His covenant faithfulness—for His forgiving our sins—for Him working righteousness in us. Although David only knew in part, Zechariah and Mary had come to understand that all these benefits from God to us come in the person and work of Jesus Christ—the one who sits on God’s eternal throne. _Let all that I am praise the LORD; with my whole heart, I will praise his holy name._ (Psalm 103:1 NLT)
